>
> **Your job: tax back-fill, nothing else.** Another session built the engine, graphs, database and tooling; do not edit anything under `apps/web`, `services`, `n8n` or `db/schema.sql`.
>
> **Goal:** for every tax year from 2015 to the latest closed one, store the four taxes the report engine reads: `CGT_FINANCIAL` (capital gains on shares and funds), `WEALTH_NET`, `WEALTH_SOLIDARITY`, `SECURITIES_ACCOUNT`, for the jurisdictions in this order: **MU, SC, NL**, then BE, DE, FR, IT, PT, ES, CH, GB, ZA. `node research/audit-country.mjs <CODE>` shows the line `report:history` ("n of 44 cells on file; missing for …"): that line is your progress meter and your definition of done. Use each country's own tax year (`apps/web/src/data/taxyear.ts`); a rate is the one in force on the tax year's first day, and mid-year changes are said in `note`. Follow the convention of the rows already stored for that tax (look at the 2024 to 2026 Netherlands rows and the 2026 rows of the country; the engine reads `headline_rate` and `threshold_amount`, and `rateForYear` picks the row that covers the tax year's first day); say in `note` where history needs a different reading.
>
> **Rules that never bend:** unknown stays unknown (never 0 unless a source says 0); every number has a source URL and the date you read it; no legal commentary; a tax that did not exist in a year is a dated **0 row whose source says so**, not an unknown; where a tax is set per region or canton (Spain's wealth tax, the Swiss wealth tax, Belgium) do not average: create the regions or cantons you can source, or leave unknown and write a gap.
>
> **Latest version wins:** for each (country, tax, tax year) the newest edition that explicitly states that year's value wins. Source ladder: (1) the tax authority's own per-year page or table (for example `belastingdienst.nl/.../fisin20XX/...`); (2) a compiled series that states the year, for example the OECD Tax Database, newest edition; (3) a dated Wayback snapshot of PwC, EY or KPMG nearest the end of that tax year (the Internet Archive was offline on 2026-10-03: test its CDX API first); (4) otherwise leave it unknown with a `gaps` entry saying what to download and where to put it (`PDFs/`). A page that shows only today's rate is not evidence for an earlier year. Never fill a gap from an older edition. If two sources disagree on a year, store the newer one with `"nv": true` and name both in `note`.
>
> **Per country:** `node research/audit-country.mjs <CODE>` → write `research/runs/<date>-<CODE>-2015.json` (shape in the header of `research/apply-run.mjs`; closed back-year rows have `from` and `to`) → `node research/apply-run.mjs <run>`, which prints the **RESTATED old → new** list (stored values this run would change) → **show Hentus a diff table (tax, tax year, rate, threshold, source, date, and the RESTATED list) and wait for his go-ahead** before `bash db/apply.sh <sql>`. Then audit again (the `report:history` line must move), run `node --test research/*.test.mjs` and `cd apps/web && npx vitest run`, and commit the run and SQL (push only when Hentus says).
>
> **Check yourself:** after each country, re-read three random cells per year from a different page or source than the one you used and list any disagreement. Report at the end of each country: cells filled, unknown, `nv`, gaps, and the regime changes you mapped (one line each).
>
> If a regime cannot be expressed by one headline rate and one threshold per year (progressive bands, deemed-return systems), do not force it: write what you stored and why in `note`, and tell Hentus in plain words.
